Long-term neurological resilience

Maintenance view (non-disease): supportive factors that help preserve reserve over time.
Maintenance view
This section is written for someone who is currently well. It is not a forecast and does not imply future symptoms.

Question this diagram addresses: If neural structure is intact, what supports long-term resilience and stability?

Current state
  • Normal neurological function
  • Stable signal conduction
  • No functional impairment
Biological capacities that support resilience
  • Mitochondrial efficiency — supports cellular energy availability
  • Myelin lipid availability — supports long-term signal insulation
  • Oxidative balance — limits cumulative cellular stress
  • Oligodendrocyte longevity — supports stable myelin maintenance
  • OPC pool preservation — maintains future adaptive capacity
  • Axonal metabolic support — protects long-range signaling
General supports
  • Nutrition, hydration, sleep → support cellular energy balance
  • Antioxidant balance → support oxidative load management
  • Structural lipid availability → membrane integrity and myelin-associated components Includes dietary sources already in use, with the optional addition of nervonic acid as a structural lipid. This does not initiate repair or differentiation. It is a permissive, sufficiency-based strategy. Differentiation refers to the process by which immature support cells (oligodendrocyte precursor cells) mature into myelin-producing cells.
  • Stable movement and activity rhythms → helps maintain normal signaling pathways through routine use, rest, and recovery
Note: This diagram focuses on maintaining neurological resilience in a healthy individual. It does not represent a disease pathway and does not describe progression or risk.
